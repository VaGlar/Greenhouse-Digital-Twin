# Climate control assumptions

`config/greenhouse_example.yaml` → `climate_control:` block. Model code: `twin/climate_model.py`.

| Parameter | Value | Tag | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `heating_setpoint_day_c` | 20.0 | **SOURCED** | Within the commonly cited optimal daytime range for greenhouse tomato, ~21–27°C is often quoted as ideal but 20°C is a standard, slightly conservative operational setpoint used in practice (heating cost vs. growth trade-off). Source: general greenhouse tomato temperature guidance, e.g. [Review of optimum temperature/VPD for greenhouse tomato](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323225604_Review_of_optimum_temperature_humidity_and_vapour_pressure_deficit_for_microclimate_evaluation_and_control_in_greenhouse_cultivation_of_tomato_a_review) (review article, no single page — synthesizes many studies). |
| `heating_setpoint_night_c` | 17.0 | **SOURCED** | Matches commonly cited optimal nighttime range of 17–18°C for greenhouse tomato. Same source as above. |
| `day_start_hour` / `day_end_hour` | 6 / 20 | **PLACEHOLDER** | A generic 14-hour "day" window, not tied to actual sunrise/sunset for the greenhouse's real latitude/season — the weather model already computes solar radiation seasonally, but this day/night climate-control window is currently a flat clock-time boundary, not linked to actual daylight. Worth revisiting: could be derived from latitude + day-of-year instead of hardcoded hours. **User-adjustable in the frontend** (2026-08-21) as the thermal screen's schedule (see `screen_energy_saving_fraction` below) — the same window also gates heating and CO2 setpoints, so this one control genuinely does triple duty. |
| `vent_temp_margin_c` | 2.0 | **PLACEHOLDER** | A standard control-engineering choice (ventilation kicks in a couple degrees above the heating setpoint to avoid heating/venting oscillating against each other) — reasonable practice, not from a specific greenhouse-tomato source. |
| `vent_max_ach` | 15.0 | **PLACEHOLDER** | Air changes per hour under full ventilation — order-of-magnitude plausible for a naturally/mechanically vented greenhouse, not benchmarked against this greenhouse's actual vent design (roof vent area, fan capacity). |
| `vent_min_ach` | 0.5 | **PLACEHOLDER** | Baseline leakage rate — generic assumption, not measured. |
| `co2_setpoint_day_ppm` | 900.0 | **SOURCED** | Falls within the commonly cited optimal CO2 enrichment range (700–1000 ppm) for greenhouse tomato photosynthesis. Same review source as temperature setpoints above. |
| `co2_ambient_ppm` | 420.0 | **SOURCED — factual, not a modeling choice** | Current global atmospheric CO2 concentration is ~420-425 ppm as of the mid-2020s (NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory / Mauna Loa Observatory record). This is a real, current value, not an assumption to recalibrate. |
| `effective_heat_capacity_j_m2k` | 40000.0 | **PLACEHOLDER** | Lumped thermal mass per m² floor area representing the air + immediate structure/crop thermal buffering — an engineering simplification (the real greenhouse has separate air, soil/substrate, and structural thermal masses that this single-zone model collapses into one number). Not sourced to a specific reference; a reasonable order-of-magnitude guess for a hydroponic (low soil mass) system, should be checked against actual measured temperature response once the greenhouse exists. |
| `screen_energy_saving_fraction` | 0.55 (added 2026-08-20) | **SOURCED** | Real thermal screen product spec from a real vendor quote for this greenhouse (type PH55, "shading-energy" screen): 55% shading, ~55% energy saving. Modeled as deployed every night (retracted during the day, per typical real operating practice — see `twin/climate_model.py` docstring), cutting transmission heat loss by this fraction whenever `not is_daytime(hour)`. Source: `papers/geothermiki-s192g-quote.md`. **Not user-adjustable** (fixed 2026-08-21, was briefly exposed as a frontend slider by mistake): this is a physical spec of the installed screen product, not an operational decision — a grower doesn't choose "how much energy the screen saves" any more than they'd choose a light bulb's wattage. The only real control is *when* the screen deploys, i.e. the day/night schedule (`day_start_hour`/`day_end_hour` above), which is what the frontend now exposes for this zone instead. |
| `cover_surface_temp_fraction` | 0.3 (added 2026-08-20) | **PLACEHOLDER** | How far the cover's *inside* surface temperature sits between outdoor and indoor air temperature (`T_cover = T_out + (T_in - T_out) * fraction`). 0.3 means the cover sits closer to outdoor temperature, reflecting that a thin PE film has little thermal resistance of its own — most of the resistance (and therefore the temperature drop) is in the air boundary layers on each side, not the plastic. The *mechanism* (condensation when a surface is below the air's dew point) is sourced (`papers/greenhouse-condensation-dewpoint-physics.md`), but this specific fraction is an engineering approximation, not measured. |
| `condensation_rate_constant` | 2.0/hour (added 2026-08-20) | **PLACEHOLDER** | How fast bulk greenhouse air's vapor pressure relaxes toward what the cold cover surface allows, once condensation conditions are met — an exponential relaxation, structurally identical to how ventilation pulls CO2/vapor toward outdoor levels. Not a measured mass-transfer coefficient; an engineering approximation. |
| `dehumidification_setpoint_pct` | 70.0 (was 85.0, corrected 2026-08-20) | **SOURCED** | RH ceiling the (idealized) active dehumidification system targets, representing the real quote's OptiClima cooling/dehumidification panels — no real capacity number was available in the quote, so this is modeled as **unconstrained** (always able to reach the setpoint), a simplification flagged in `README.md`'s open-gaps list. Literature range for greenhouse tomato is 60-85% (day 80-85%, night 65-75%), but **70% is specifically cited as the pollination optimum** (above 80%, pollen grains stick together and disperse poorly; below 60%, the stigma dries out) — used as the single setpoint value since it's the one figure in the range literature calls out as an actual optimum rather than an endpoint. Source: [DryGair — What are the Ideal Conditions for Greenhouse Tomatoes?](https://drygair.com/blog/what-are-the-ideal-conditions-for-greenhouse-tomatoes/) (web article, no page number). **Finding (2026-08-20, before this correction):** once VPD was wired into the photosynthesis response (see `crop-model.md`), a sensitivity check at an earlier, higher 85% guess showed yield rises substantially as the setpoint tightens — 85%→8.4 kg/m², 80%→10.8, 75%→13.2, 70%→15.5, 65%→17.5 kg/m² (150-day run) — because a humid ceiling keeps VPD below its ~0.85 kPa photosynthetic optimum. At the corrected 70% setpoint, mean VPD lands at ~0.88 kPa (right at the optimum) and yield is 15.49 kg/m². |

## Modeling structure note (not a numeric assumption)

The climate model's core architecture — CHP heat/CO2 as a *fixed supply the greenhouse draws from, with surplus dumped/vented rather than the CHP throttling to match demand* — is a confirmed real design decision from the user (the CHP runs at constant electrical output, grid-driven), not a literature assumption. See `twin/climate_model.py` module docstring.
