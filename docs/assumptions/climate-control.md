# Climate control assumptions

`config/greenhouse_example.yaml` → `climate_control:` block. Model code: `twin/climate_model.py`.

| Parameter | Value | Tag | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `heating_setpoint_day_c` | 20.0 | **SOURCED** | Within the commonly cited optimal daytime range for greenhouse tomato, ~21–27°C is often quoted as ideal but 20°C is a standard, slightly conservative operational setpoint used in practice (heating cost vs. growth trade-off). Source: general greenhouse tomato temperature guidance, e.g. [Review of optimum temperature/VPD for greenhouse tomato](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323225604_Review_of_optimum_temperature_humidity_and_vapour_pressure_deficit_for_microclimate_evaluation_and_control_in_greenhouse_cultivation_of_tomato_a_review) (review article, no single page — synthesizes many studies). |
| `heating_setpoint_night_c` | 17.0 | **SOURCED** | Matches commonly cited optimal nighttime range of 17–18°C for greenhouse tomato. Same source as above. |
| `day_start_hour` / `day_end_hour` | 6 / 20 | **PLACEHOLDER** | A generic 14-hour "day" window, not tied to actual sunrise/sunset for the greenhouse's real latitude/season — the weather model already computes solar radiation seasonally, but this day/night climate-control window is currently a flat clock-time boundary, not linked to actual daylight. Gates the heating setpoint switch and CO2 dosing target, and is one of the thermal screen's night trigger (see `screen_energy_saving_fraction` below). **Not user-adjustable.** Worth revisiting: could be derived from latitude + day-of-year instead of hardcoded hours. |
| `vent_temp_margin_c` | 2.0 | **PLACEHOLDER** | A standard control-engineering choice (ventilation kicks in a couple degrees above the heating setpoint to avoid heating/venting oscillating against each other) — reasonable practice, not from a specific greenhouse-tomato source. |
| `vent_max_ach` | 15.0 | **PLACEHOLDER** | Air changes per hour under full ventilation — order-of-magnitude plausible for a naturally/mechanically vented greenhouse, not benchmarked against this greenhouse's actual vent design (roof vent area, fan capacity). |
| `vent_min_ach` | 0.5 | **PLACEHOLDER** | Baseline leakage rate — generic assumption, not measured. |
| `co2_setpoint_day_ppm` | 900.0 | **SOURCED** | Falls within the commonly cited optimal CO2 enrichment range (700–1000 ppm) for greenhouse tomato photosynthesis. Same review source as temperature setpoints above. |
| `co2_ambient_ppm` | 420.0 | **SOURCED — factual, not a modeling choice** | Current global atmospheric CO2 concentration is ~420-425 ppm as of the mid-2020s (NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory / Mauna Loa Observatory record). This is a real, current value, not an assumption to recalibrate. |
| `effective_heat_capacity_j_m2k` | 40000.0 | **PLACEHOLDER** | Lumped thermal mass per m² floor area representing the air + immediate structure/crop thermal buffering — an engineering simplification (the real greenhouse has separate air, soil/substrate, and structural thermal masses that this single-zone model collapses into one number). Not sourced to a specific reference; a reasonable order-of-magnitude guess for a hydroponic (low soil mass) system, should be checked against actual measured temperature response once the greenhouse exists. |
| `screen_energy_saving_fraction` | 0.55 (added 2026-08-20) | **SOURCED** | Real thermal screen product spec from a real vendor quote for this greenhouse (type PH55, "shading-energy" screen): **55% shading AND ~55% energy saving — the same fabric, same figure, for both effects.** Source: `papers/geothermiki-s192g-quote.md`. Cuts transmission heat loss **and** solar gain (both climate model and crop model's photosynthesis light input) by this fraction whenever deployed. **Not user-adjustable** — a physical spec of the installed product, not an operational decision. **Deployment is fully automatic** (`twin/climate_model.py`: `GreenhouseClimateModel.decide_screen_deployment`), redesigned 2026-08-24 to match how a real screen controller actually behaves, per 3 criteria evaluated every hour, first match wins: (1) **night** — outside `day_start_hour`/`day_end_hour`; (2) **too hot** — see `VENT_RAMP_BAND_C` below for how this is actually checked (via `_ventilated_temp`, not a fixed margin); (3) **cold + CHP insufficient, with a cost/benefit check** — required heating power exceeds `chp_heat_margin_fraction` (90%) of the CHP's fixed heat output, **and** the transmission-loss savings the screen would provide exceed the solar-gain loss from shading at that hour (both computed directly from that hour's `q_trans_loss_w`/`q_solar_w`, scaled by `screen_energy_saving_fraction`) — otherwise leaves it retracted, since on a cold-but-sunny hour shading can lose more free solar heat than the insulation saves. |
| `chp_heat_margin_fraction` | 0.9 (added 2026-08-24) | **PLACEHOLDER** | Safety margin for the screen's cold-trigger (see above) — reacts once required heating power hits 90% of the CHP's fixed output rather than waiting for 100% (which would mean the setpoint is already being missed before the screen even responds). An engineering safety-margin choice, not from a specific literature source. |
| `VENT_RAMP_BAND_C` (`twin/climate_model.py` constant, not YAML-configurable) | 5.0°C | **PLACEHOLDER** | Degrees above `vent_setpoint` over which ventilation ramps from `vent_min_ach` to `vent_max_ach`. **Two bugs found & fixed here, 2026-08-24 then 2026-08-25:** (1) 2026-08-24 — the screen's "too hot" trigger initially compared the raw pre-ventilation projected temperature to `vent_setpoint` alone (the point ventilation merely *starts* ramping, not where it's fully engaged) — shaded ~59% of all daytime hours for no real benefit, cut yield ~17%. Widened to `vent_setpoint + VENT_RAMP_BAND_C` as an interim fix. (2) 2026-08-25 — that interim fix was itself found to be comparing against the wrong thing: a **projection with zero ventilation applied at all**, when what actually matters is whether *fully-ramped* ventilation would be enough. Real weather data (see `weather.md`) exposed this starkly: 70% of all daylight hours were being shaded, and — far more seriously — the same "project with no ventilation, then apply a full hour of ventilation removal in one linear step" pattern in the *main* energy balance (not just the screen check) caused ventilation to badly **overshoot past `vent_setpoint` and crash indoor air temperature all the way down to outdoor air** on cold-but-sunny hours (confirmed: indoor hit exactly 3.91°C, equal to outdoor, misread at the time as "the CHP is too small" — it wasn't; the CHP never exceeded 747 of its 1150kW capacity in that run). Fixed by adding `GreenhouseClimateModel._ventilated_temp`, a shared helper both the main step's ventilation and the screen's "too hot" check now use: it floors the post-ventilation temperature at `max(temp_out_c, vent_setpoint)` whenever *active* (above-baseline) ventilation is engaged, matching how a real thermostatic vent controller stops removing heat once it reaches its target rather than continuing until indoor matches outdoor. Baseline leakage ventilation (`vent_min_ach`, always present) is left alone — that's passive exchange, not active regulation, and should keep drifting toward outdoor air normally. Impact on the real-weather 150-day run: min indoor temp 3.91°C → 15.56°C, daylight hours shaded 70% → 14%, yield 14.11 → 32.79 kg/m² (see `weather.md` for the full before/after). |
| `cover_surface_temp_fraction` | 0.3 (added 2026-08-20) | **PLACEHOLDER** | How far the cover's *inside* surface temperature sits between outdoor and indoor air temperature (`T_cover = T_out + (T_in - T_out) * fraction`). 0.3 means the cover sits closer to outdoor temperature, reflecting that a thin PE film has little thermal resistance of its own — most of the resistance (and therefore the temperature drop) is in the air boundary layers on each side, not the plastic. The *mechanism* (condensation when a surface is below the air's dew point) is sourced (`papers/greenhouse-condensation-dewpoint-physics.md`), but this specific fraction is an engineering approximation, not measured. |
| `condensation_rate_constant` | 2.0/hour (added 2026-08-20) | **PLACEHOLDER** | How fast bulk greenhouse air's vapor pressure relaxes toward what the cold cover surface allows, once condensation conditions are met — an exponential relaxation, structurally identical to how ventilation pulls CO2/vapor toward outdoor levels. Not a measured mass-transfer coefficient; an engineering approximation. |
| `dehumidification_setpoint_pct` | 70.0 (was 85.0, corrected 2026-08-20) | **SOURCED** | RH ceiling the (idealized) active dehumidification system targets, representing the real quote's OptiClima cooling/dehumidification panels — no real capacity number was available in the quote, so this is modeled as **unconstrained** (always able to reach the setpoint), a simplification flagged in `README.md`'s open-gaps list. Literature range for greenhouse tomato is 60-85% (day 80-85%, night 65-75%), but **70% is specifically cited as the pollination optimum** (above 80%, pollen grains stick together and disperse poorly; below 60%, the stigma dries out) — used as the single setpoint value since it's the one figure in the range literature calls out as an actual optimum rather than an endpoint. Source: [DryGair — What are the Ideal Conditions for Greenhouse Tomatoes?](https://drygair.com/blog/what-are-the-ideal-conditions-for-greenhouse-tomatoes/) (web article, no page number). **Finding (2026-08-20, before this correction):** once VPD was wired into the photosynthesis response (see `crop-model.md`), a sensitivity check at an earlier, higher 85% guess showed yield rises substantially as the setpoint tightens — 85%→8.4 kg/m², 80%→10.8, 75%→13.2, 70%→15.5, 65%→17.5 kg/m² (150-day run) — because a humid ceiling keeps VPD below its ~0.85 kPa photosynthetic optimum. At the corrected 70% setpoint, mean VPD lands at ~0.88 kPa (right at the optimum) and yield is 15.49 kg/m². |

| `fan_pad_cooling_enabled` | `false` (added 2026-08-25) | **Config choice** | Does this greenhouse have fan-and-pad evaporative cooling? Requested by the user after confirming ventilation had no fan-pad modeled and noting the vendor quote's "OptiClima cooling panels" language (`papers/geothermiki-s192g-quote.md`) — currently that quote's cooling panels are only mapped to `dehumidification_setpoint_pct`, not to this temperature effect; whether the real quoted system also does fan-pad-style temperature cooling (vs. only dehumidification/coil cooling) is not confirmed, so this defaults **off** until that's known for the actual installed system. An initial on/off choice in the config, not a literature-sourced parameter itself. |
| `fan_pad_efficiency` | 0.80 (added 2026-08-25) | **SOURCED** | Fraction of the way pad-cooled air moves from raw outdoor dry-bulb conditions toward saturation at the outdoor wet-bulb temperature. UF/IFAS Extension (CIR1135/AE069) cites up to 85% for a well-designed, well-maintained system; Alabama Extension cites 70-85% generally for well-maintained systems (with a wider real-world range of 58-85% across studies). 0.80 sits within the well-maintained sub-range. Source: `papers/fan-pad-evaporative-cooling.md`. Only used when `fan_pad_cooling_enabled` is true. |
| `_wet_bulb_temp_c` (`twin/climate_model.py` function, not YAML-configurable) | Stull (2011) formula | **SOURCED** | Empirical approximation of wet-bulb temperature from dry-bulb temperature + RH, avoiding an iterative psychrometric solve. Standard meteorological formula. Source: `papers/fan-pad-evaporative-cooling.md`. |
| `_fan_pad_outdoor_conditions` mechanism (`twin/climate_model.py`, not YAML-configurable) | — | **Modeling structure note** | When `fan_pad_cooling_enabled` and ventilation is actively cooling (`ach > vent_min_ach`), both the temperature *and* the vapor pressure of the "outdoor" air ventilation exchanges with are moved the same `fan_pad_efficiency` fraction of the way toward the wet-bulb point (temperature down toward wet-bulb, vapor pressure up toward saturation at wet-bulb) — modeled together, not just the cooling side, since the pad process is adiabatic: the same evaporation that removes heat is what adds moisture, so a real fan-pad system that cools the air necessarily also humidifies it. This lets `_ventilated_temp` cool below raw outdoor dry-bulb temperature (the whole point of the feature) while the humidity balance sees a correspondingly wetter incoming airstream, rather than getting the cooling benefit "for free." Baseline leakage ventilation (`vent_min_ach`) is left on raw outdoor air, matching how a real system only runs its pads alongside its exhaust fans. The screen's "too hot" check (`decide_screen_deployment`) uses the same pad-adjusted temperature (at max ACH) so a fan-pad-equipped greenhouse doesn't shade unnecessarily on a hot-but-dry day the pads alone could handle. Verified in a 150-day real-weather run: enabling the toggle drops max indoor temp from 30.4°C to 23.1°C and raises mean indoor RH from 57.6% to 58.3%, with yield essentially unchanged (32.79 → 32.70 kg/m²) since the modeled cooling here mostly just avoids a max-vent/screen trade-off rather than unlocking new growth. |

## Modeling structure note (not a numeric assumption)

The climate model's core architecture — CHP heat/CO2 as a *fixed supply the greenhouse draws from, with surplus dumped/vented rather than the CHP throttling to match demand* — is a confirmed real design decision from the user (the CHP runs at constant electrical output, grid-driven), not a literature assumption. See `twin/climate_model.py` module docstring.

**Heat consumption is already hard-capped at the CHP's max output** (`twin/climate_model.py`: `heat_used_w = min(required_w, heat_available_w)`) — the greenhouse can never draw more heat than `chp.heat_available_kw` (`electric_power_kw * heat_to_power_ratio`, 1150 kW at the current config) in any hour, regardless of how cold it is outside or how the setpoints are configured; any shortfall just means the setpoint isn't fully reached that hour. The frontend's "Θερμική κατανάλωση" chart (added 2026-08-24, normalized to daily-average kW rather than daily total kWh so it's a rate directly comparable to that cap) plots this max as a reference line.
